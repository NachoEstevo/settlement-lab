# Settlement volume and waiting time

```sh
./gradlew -q run --args="benchmark economic 20261119 32"
```

The command writes one compact JSON report to standard output. The arguments select the workload seed and the queue window (1–128). The defaults are 20261119 and 32. Save stdout to retain the opening balances, every scheduled payment and cancellation, the decisions for both policies, and the status of unpaid obligations at the cutoff. The full input fixtures are generated by [EconomicWorkloads](../src/main/java/lab/settlement/benchmark/EconomicWorkloads.java).

Every workload has eight logical ticks. At each tick, the benchmark submits the scheduled arrivals, requests the scheduled cancellations in order, then gives each policy **one** chance to settle one group from the first W queued payments. An empty selection consumes that opportunity. The run stops when tick eight ends, including any payments still in the queue. A cancellation request sent to both policies can succeed in one run and find an already settled obligation in the other.

## Measured results

These are full runs at seed 20261119 and W=32. Amounts are whole SIM units. Age is the cutoff tick minus the arrival tick. An obligation settled on arrival has waited zero ticks.

| Workload | Opening cash | Policy | Settled count | Gross settled | Cancelled | Pending | Oldest pending (ticks) |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Liquid | 4,000 | FIFO | 56 | 308 | 1 | 0 | — |
| Liquid | 4,000 | Heuristic | 56 | 308 | 1 | 0 | — |
| Blocked rings | 60 | FIFO | 0 | 0 | 1 | 23 | 7 |
| Blocked rings | 60 | Heuristic | 24 | 1,995 | 0 | 0 | — |
| Asymmetric | 5 | FIFO | 1 | 4 | 0 | 15 | 7 |
| Asymmetric | 5 | Heuristic | 15 | 295 | 0 | 1 | 6 |

**Liquid.** All four accounts open with enough cash for their outgoing obligations. The first burst contains 36 payments. Both policies settle the visible 32, and the customer cancels payment 36 before the next opportunity. At W=1 both policies settle eight payments in eight ticks and leave 48 queued.

**Blocked rings.** The first three payments form A→B→C→A for 100 each. Each account opens with 20. FIFO stops at A's first payment. The heuristic settles all three for 300 gross. The third-tick request to cancel the first C→A payment succeeds under FIFO and returns `ALREADY_SETTLED` under the heuristic. With W=2, the heuristic never sees all three legs at once and settles zero over the eight ticks.

**Asymmetric.** A opens with 5 and owes B 4 followed by C 5. At the first opportunity the heuristic drops A's newest outgoing payment and settles 4. Settling only the second payment would yield 5. The exact first-window gap is 1, or 20% of the optimum of 5. The [independent oracle tests](../src/test/java/lab/settlement/selection/SelectionPolicyTest.java) check this counterexample. Subsequent ticks bring other obligations. Across the full heuristic run, A settles one payment on arrival and another after one tick. B and C settle seven and six on arrival, respectively. D's outgoing payment for 200 remains unpaid for six ticks, even as other participants settle 295 gross.

## Reading the report

The `schedule` is shared by both policy runs. Every payment has a domain-local ID, sender, recipient, amount and admission sequence. Each `decisions` entry contains the tick, observed revision, inspected count, continuation flag (`hasMore`), cancellation results, selected payment IDs, their wait in ticks and the settled gross. Match the IDs to the schedule to reconstruct the batch. The report includes each pending ID and age at the cutoff. The per-sender histograms map elapsed ticks to the number of outgoing obligations that settled or remained queued.

The measurements above used Java 21.0.7 on Windows x64 with an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X. The harness runs the production selectors and in-memory ledger serially, with synthetic inputs and no external I/O during selection. It measures economic results in logical time. The PostgreSQL capacity experiment measures elapsed time and contention with independent worker connections.
